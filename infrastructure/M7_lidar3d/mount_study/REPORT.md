# 3D LiDAR mount study (geometric characterization before M8)

Status: characterization only. The default sensor configuration, the environment and the policies are unchanged.

## 1. Question and constraints

Find the mount height z and pitch that give the most useful view for navigation. Objectives, in order:
1. no rays escaping above the walls;
2. as few floor returns as possible;
3. useful wall and obstacle coverage at all distances;
4. no self-occlusion.

Fixed: x = y = 0, roll = yaw = 0, the full FoV (28° × 14°, 34 × 139 = 4,726 rays, cell centres), 4 Hz, 12 m range,
and the environment (walls and baffles 0.6 m, moving obstacles 0.5 m tall, r = 0.17 m).

Searched: z = 0.36–0.60 m in base_link, in 2 cm steps. The top platform is at 0.350 m, so 0.36 m is the sensor almost
touching the plate; 0.60 m is the wall height. Pitch 0–16° (down = +) in 0.25° steps. 845 candidates.

## 2. Method

* **Returns:** computed with the analytic 3D oracle (`st_nav.geometry.analytic3d`). In M7 it matched the GPU mesh ray
  caster to < 0.01 mm on walls and floor, and to 0.75 mm median on the obstacle cylinders. No simulator is needed.
  Scripts are in `scripts/`; results and figures in `results/`.
* **Poses** (every candidate sees the same poses and scene states):
  * **trajectory poses:** robot pose plus that episode's baffles or obstacle positions at the same step, from the
    recorded evaluations of trained policies. Static: NAV01 `ref_static_c10`, 100 episodes. Dynamic: NAV03
    `ref_chase_c10`, 100 episodes, OB1 + OB2 moving;
  * **random poses:** uniform position with footprint clearance ≥ 0.227 m and uniform heading, to cover orientations the
    policy rarely takes (facing walls, looking down long corridors).
  * Grid sweep: every 10th step + 500 random poses per scene (≈ 2,000 per scene). Shortlist: every 2nd step + 5,000
    random poses (12,228 static and 13,557 dynamic poses).
* **Robot attitude:** measured in the simulator with the trained NAV01 and NAV03 policies (`results/base_attitude_*.json`,
  358k samples each).
  * base_link sits 4.5 mm below the floor plane and pitched 0.252° nose-up, varying by only ±0.01° while driving;
  * rare events in the static scene (0.1 % of samples) reach 1.31° nose-up;
  * every candidate is evaluated at the nominal attitude, and its escapes again at the worst observed tilt (1.31° nose-up).
* **Ray classes** (exclusive): floor, wall, static obstacle (Z1–Z4 baffles), moving obstacle, no return.
* **Escape** (overlaps the classes): the ray passes above a wall or baffle within 12 m, i.e. an infinitely tall wall
  would have stopped it. Every escape observed here leaves the arena over an outer wall and returns nothing, so
  "escape" = "no return".
* **Top-face:** a return from the top face of a wall (z = 0.6 m) or of a moving obstacle (0.5 m), a ray one step from
  passing over it.
* **Self-occlusion:** all rays cast against every TB4 base_link mesh triangle (visual and collision).

## 3. Why zero escapes and zero floor returns is impossible

The vertical fan is 13.59° tall (bottom to top row) and must fit between the floor (0 m) and the wall top (0.6 m). It
fits only up to about **D\* = 2.5 m** from the robot, whatever the height:
* at mount height h, D\* solves atan(h / D\*) + atan((0.6 − h) / D\*) = 13.59°;
* for h between 0.40 and 0.50 m, D\* = 2.5 m.

Beyond that distance, the top row is above the walls or the bottom row is on the floor. Free lines of sight longer
than 2.5 m are common (corridors, and the arena diagonal of 8.02 m): 51–69 % of the poses have floor returns even for
the best candidates.

The trade-off is therefore set by where the fan is aimed:
* **Zero escape for any pose:** the top row must stay below 0.6 m at the longest line of sight, i.e. top-row
  elevation ≤ atan((0.6 − h) / 8.02 m). That fixes a minimum pitch for each height (frontier in
  `results/mount_map.png`; the analytic bound and the simulated frontier agree within 0.2°).
* **Floor returns** then start at distance h / tan(bottom row) and shrink as the mount rises: a higher sensor lowers
  the fan's angle to the floor.
* **The rise is capped at 0.50 m:** above the 0.5 m moving obstacles, the upper rows pass over obstacles near the
  robot. This creates a near-field blind zone (0.21 m at z = 0.56 m), 21 % fewer rays on obstacles at 0.5–1 m, and
  top-face returns.
* **Self-occlusion** limits low mounts: z = 0.36 m at every pitch, z = 0.38 m from 6°, z = 0.40 m from 13.75°.

## 5. Ranking and recommendation

Ranking criteria, applied in this order:
1. zero escape, both at the nominal attitude and at the worst observed tilt;
2. no loss of obstacle coverage (no blind zone, no top-face returns), as part of objective 3;
3. least floor;
4. no self-occlusion.

1. **R1: z = 0.50 m (15 cm above the top platform), pitch 7.5° down.** Recommended.
   * No escapes, at the nominal attitude and at the worst tilt; the top row is 0.45° below horizontal.
   * No top-face returns, no blind zone; obstacle coverage the same as today (3,408 / 2,265 rays at 0.5–1 / 1–2 m).
   * Lowest floor fraction (8.2 % static / 12.8 % dynamic) of all candidates that satisfy objective 1 robustly without
     losing coverage.
   * Its 7.5° pitch leaves 0.14° of margin over the 7.36° analytic bound at the worst tilt.
2. **R3: z = 0.46 m, 7.25°** (10.0 / 14.5 % floor). The same properties with a 4 cm shorter standoff.
3. **R4: z = 0.40 m (current height), 6.75°** (12.8 / 17.2 % floor). The choice if the sensor must stay at its current
   height: only the tilt changes, from 5° to 6.75°.
4. **R2: z = 0.56 m, 8°** (6.5 / 11.1 % floor). Fewest floor returns among the robust candidates, but it fails objective 3:
   * 0.21 m near-field blind zone for the 0.5 m obstacles;
   * 21 % fewer obstacle rays at 0.5–1 m;
   * 0.65 % of rays hit obstacle tops.

   Higher mounts are worse on all three.

If only the nominal attitude must be escape-free (a margin of 0.2°–0.3°), N1 (0.50 m, 6.5°) has the fewest floor
returns, 5.9 / 10.1 %: below the current mount, without its escapes. It still escapes 0.19 / 0.51 % at the worst tilt.

**Cost of fixing escapes.** Removing escapes costs floor returns: R1 has 0.8 / 1.5 points more floor than the current
mount. The current mount's escapes reach 9–11 % of poses, and 0.3–0.7 % of rays at the worst tilt.

**Choice for you.** The simulated robot barely tilts (±0.01° while driving). The real TurtleBot4 will rock more, and
the real walls may not be 0.6 m. R1 has the larger margin; N1 has fewer floor returns. Raising the sensor to 0.50 m
needs a 15 cm standoff on the top platform; whether that is practical for the Mirrorcle housing is your call.

## 7. Limitations

* **Geometry only:** no noise, beam divergence or material effects. Rays hitting a wall's top edge are counted
  exactly. The oracle's obstacle cylinders are exact circles, whereas the simulator uses 32 facets.
* **Robot attitude** comes from the simulator (rigid robot, flat floor). Real pitch and roll, mount tolerances and
  calibration error will be larger; the worst-tilt column is only a proxy.
* **Poses** come from two trained policies plus uniform random poses. A different policy may favour other headings.
  The analytic bound (top row below 0.6 m at 8.02 m) holds for every pose.
* **Physical feasibility of a mount height** (standoff, housing, centre of mass) has not been assessed.

in a recorded NAV03 episode (episode 56, both moving obstacles in view at step 15). It contains the mount views, the

**At the selected frame.** Both mounts see the same obstacle area: 1,205 / 1,039 rays (R1) vs 1,202 / 1,042 (R4) on

about 1° would bring the lowest rays onto the plate edge.

## 10. Part 2: forward offset, between-deck heights and a narrower vertical FoV

Question: can a Mirrorcle placed forward, at about the original 2D LiDAR height, see better with less downward pitch?

**Constraints**

* Sweep variables: x offset, height z, pitch and vertical FoV. Fixed: y = roll = yaw = 0, the 28° horizontal FoV and
  the 139 × 34 grid (a narrower vertical FoV samples more finely).
* Scripts: `scripts/mount_sweep_xzfov.py`, tables `results/tables_xzfov.md`, renders `results/visual_comparison_xzfov/`.

**Geometry** (TB4 meshes):

* Original 2D LiDAR (RPLIDAR) optical centre: (−0.040, 0, 0.193) m in base_link, **below** the top platform (surface
  0.350 m, underside 0.344 m). There is no discrepancy: the sweep runs between the decks, from 0.193 m upwards.
* Mirrorcle enclosure 85 × 84 × 73 mm. Assumed: 85 along x, 84 along y, 73 tall, with the optical centre at its centre.
  It is checked against every robot mesh vertex, the plate underside and the footprint (base shell, r = 0.177 m).
  * **z:** from 0.193 m up to 0.298 m. At 0.298 m the enclosure top is 1 cm under the plate. The base structures in
    front of the RPLIDAR reach at most 0.152 m.
  * **x:** at x = 0 the enclosure collides with the RPLIDAR (z ≤ 0.23 m). From x = 0.043 m every height fits.
    At x = 0.129 m the enclosure's front corners touch the footprint edge (no margin).
  * **Self-occlusion:** none of the 259 configurations has any (rays cast against all robot triangles, including
    wheels and posts).

**Sweep**

* Coarse grid: x {0, 0.043, 0.086, 0.129} × z {0.193, 0.230, 0.265, 0.298} × pitch {1.5, 2.5, 3.5, 5}° × vertical FoV
  {14, 12, 10, 8}°, plus the original mount, R4 and R1. That is 256 + 3 configurations, on about 2,000 poses per scene.
* Refinement: every height and FoV at x = 0.086 and 0.129 m, at the smallest 0.25° pitch that guarantees zero escapes
  for any pose at the worst observed tilt (analytic bound). Run on the dense pose set (12,228 static / 13,557 dynamic).

**Results** (dense; floor static / dynamic; all refined configurations have zero escapes at both attitudes and no
self-occlusion):

**Trade-offs**

1. **The vertical FoV is the decisive variable.** With the full 14°, a between-deck mount must be pitched 5.25–6° down
   to stay escape-free. Its lower height then brings the floor closer: 19–35 % floor, worse than R1. Only a narrower
   vertical FoV lowers the required pitch (to 3.25° at 8°, 4° at 10°) and with it the floor fraction.
2. **Height:** higher is better, as before: the floor appears later. The best between-deck height is the highest that
   fits, 0.298 m. At the original 2D LiDAR height (0.193 m), even 8° only matches R1.
3. **x offset: small effect, and not free.** Moving from x = 0 to 0.129 m lowers floor by about 1.5 points and adds
   about 5 % more rays on near obstacles. It also moves the fan's apex forward, so the full robot width is covered only
   from 0.62–0.66 m ahead of the front instead of 0.53 m. At 0.129 m the enclosure has no footprint margin, so 0.086 m
   is preferable.
4. **What a narrower FoV costs.** The fan is a thinner horizontal band. At 1 m it spans 14 cm (8°) or 17 cm (10°) of
   height, against 24 cm (14°); a wall 4 m away is 59–62 % covered, against 77 % (R1).

   In exchange the rows are denser (0.235° at 8° vs 0.412°). Obstacles therefore get as many or more rays: +4 % at
   0.5–1 m, +20 % at 2–3 m, +30 % beyond 3 m. There is no vertical blind zone either, since the sensor is below the
   obstacle tops.

   All objects in this environment rise from the floor to at least 0.5 m, so nothing in it is missed. Objects lower
   or higher than the band at a given distance (low clutter, overhangs, tables) would be missed in the real world.

**Do they help?**

* **B8 (x 0.086, z 0.298, pitch 3.25°, vertical FoV 8°)** is a meaningful geometric improvement in this environment:
  * floor returns are less than half of R1's (3.2 / 6.7 % vs 8.2 / 12.8 %);
  * the floor starts at 2.4 m instead of 2.0 m;
  * zero escapes, even at the worst tilt; obstacles get more rays.

  It comes with three conditions: an 8° vertical scan, a bracket under the top plate with 1 cm of clearance (enclosure
  assumed centred on the optical centre), and a thinner vertical band.
* **B10 (pitch 4°, 10°)** is only about 15 % better than R1 on floor, with 30 % less vertical coverage than R1. It is
  not a clear gain.
* **Keeping the full 14° FoV:** moving the sensor forward or down to the original LiDAR height does not help. R1 (and
  R4 at the current height) remain the better choices.
